# Falsification Framework — Pass 36: Self-Modification and Meta-Organ

## Distinctive claim under test

A persistent federation of heterogeneous specialist organs can safely modify its own roles, population and communication topology in response to task experience, producing causal gains beyond strong static and learned-routing alternatives without unacceptable safety, privacy, capability-retention or recovery regressions.

## Direct falsifiers

The claim is weakened or falsified in the tested domain if any of the following holds:

1. Runtime mutations do not outperform a strong task-conditioned initial planner on paired tasks.
2. Apparent gains disappear after matching tokens, model calls, latency, information and verifier budgets.
3. A sham extra planning pass produces the same gains as actual topology edits.
4. Improvements occur only with homogeneous role prompts and do not transfer to genuinely heterogeneous specialist models.
5. Role generation changes labels/prompts but not measurable capability profiles or unique information contribution.
6. Persistent evolution improves the training/task stream but harms held-out tasks or protected capabilities.
7. The system's evaluator can be gamed, or the proposer can modify evaluation criteria, permissions or rollback authority without independent approval.
8. Safety regression exceeds pre-registered bounds or cannot be reliably detected before canary deployment.
9. Rollback fails to restore memory, tools, permissions, graph state or pending actions after injected faults.
10. The topology evolves toward a single dominant organ or redundant role clones, erasing the intended independent specialization.
11. A static router, workflow search method or conventional orchestration system achieves equal or better utility at lower total cost and risk.
12. No reproducible improvement remains after independent replication across model families and domains.

## Required evidence before increasing autonomy

- Paired randomized mutation/no-mutation comparisons.
- Hidden held-out tasks and externally recomputed outcomes.
- Separate capability and safety reporting.
- Independent evaluator and approval authority.
- Longitudinal retention and distribution-shift testing.
- Mutation lineage logs and blast-radius accounting.
- Verified rollback and interruption tests.
- Heterogeneous model-family replication.

## Stop conditions

Disable automatic mutation for the affected class if:
- any safety invariant is violated;
- a mutation changes its own evaluator or authorization root;
- the audit/rollback record becomes incomplete;
- an external action cannot be made idempotent or safely compensated;
- repeated regressions exceed the pre-registered limit;
- the system cannot identify the exact active configuration.

A failed mutation should revert to the last validated state, not trigger an unbounded chain of further self-modifications.
