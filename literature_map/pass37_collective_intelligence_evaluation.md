# Living Literature Map — Pass 37
**Theme:** When does a federation add collective value?

## A. Architecture scaling and task structure
- Kim et al., *Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems* (arXiv preprint): 180 configurations, task-dependent coordination; parallel finance and dynamic browsing differ from sequential planning.
- Tran & Kiela, *Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems…* (arXiv preprint): negative result under matched reasoning-token budgets on multi-hop reasoning.
- Fu et al., *Do More Agents Help?* / BenchAgent (arXiv preprint): common evaluation substrate, ten benchmarks, pooled accuracy and token accounting; only one tested evolving workflow exceeds the single-agent pooled anchor, at higher token use.
- Sanabria, Frontiers 2026: compact fully contextualized rover scenarios; no reliable aggregate multi-agent quality advantage, high overhead.

## B. Orchestration quality and information preservation
- Ren et al., OrchBench (arXiv preprint): DAG simulation, information-transfer retention, quality/makespan/token metrics; useful as screening, but real-world validation remains small.
- SC-MAS (arXiv preprint): task-conditioned role selection, pairwise edge strategies and backbone assignment; supports non-uniform collaboration patterns.

## C. Collective emergence and synergy measurement
- Riedl, ICLR 2026: PID-based higher-order coordination, alignment and synergy in a minimal guessing game; shows group structure can be measured, but task success does not automatically increase.
- Wunderlich et al., ACL 2026 SRW: bounded Pareto comparison of self-consistency, debate and mixture-of-agents; modest equal-compute gains over self-consistency on two benchmarks.

## D. Gaps directly relevant to the Holobiont
1. Few studies compare genuinely independent, independently trained specialists under matched total resources.
2. Few experiments partition unique evidence among organs and then perform ownership swaps to test causal transfer.
3. Emergence metrics need validation against external task utility and fault resilience.
4. Orchestration simulation must be validated beyond a small set of real execution traces.
5. Long-horizon persistent specialist identity and capability retention remain under-tested.
6. Resource parity should be multi-dimensional: compute, tokens, latency, memory, bandwidth, tools, information and verification.

## E. Reading priority
1. Kim et al. for task-conditioned scaling and error amplification.
2. Tran & Kiela for a strong equal-reasoning-budget negative control.
3. BenchAgent for protocol-aligned workflow comparison and answer-preservation diagnostics.
4. OrchBench for low-cost orchestration screening.
5. Riedl for information-theoretic synergy measurement.
6. SC-MAS for edge-level heterogeneity.
7. Sanabria for compact-context single-agent boundary conditions.
