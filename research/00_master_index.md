# Master Research Index

Latest committed pass: **Pass 38 — 2026-11-23**. The originating Treatise/PDF remains a hypothesis/specification, not established fact.

## Current synthesis

Pass 36 strengthens the evidence for task-conditioned role/topology adaptation and bounded runtime graph repair, while adding a critical safety correction: persistent self-evolution can create endogenous downstream misalignment and evaluator/reward hacking. The current Meta-Organ hypothesis is therefore a least-privilege proposal-and-validation service, not a sovereign self-modifier.

The strongest near-term architecture is a typed mutation pipeline: observe, diagnose, propose a bounded delta, validate schema/permissions, sandbox, independently evaluate, approve, canary, then commit or rollback. Begin with topology-only changes over a registered organ pool. Do not allow the proposer to modify its own evaluator, authorization root, safety rules or rollback system.

The full claim—safe long-horizon evolution of heterogeneous specialist organs, including new capability creation and regeneration—remains unproven. The decisive experiment is a paired runtime-mutation versus no-mutation comparison with equal budgets, independent evaluation, heterogeneous organs and longitudinal safety tests.

## Research discipline
Follow citation chains; preserve negative and null findings; distinguish peer-reviewed results from preprints; match total resources and information access; measure privacy leakage and benign false rejection separately; use randomized interventions for causal claims; distinguish answer diversity, correctness dependence, unique information recovered, and task utility; never infer Holobiont-specific synergy from message volume, consensus, entropy, or trace graphs alone.


## Pass 37 — 2026-11-22: task-conditioned collective advantage

The key question is whether a federation creates causal, resource-adjusted value beyond a strong single model, best-of-K, multi-output/self-conditioning, MoE/router and conventional MAS. New evidence is explicitly mixed: parallelizable tasks can benefit from coordination, while sequential or compact fully observed tasks can lose to a single model. Measured emergence/synergy is not automatically task utility. The strongest next step is a private-evidence partition with ownership swaps, matched multi-dimensional budgets and external verification.

## Pass 38 — 2026-11-23: effective organ independence and common-mode failure

The central refinement is to separate functional complementarity from response diversity. Complementary organs add unique capabilities or private evidence; redundant organs covering the same critical function should fail differently under matched perturbations. Model/provider count and semantic distance are not independence guarantees.

A new open-access journal article gives a variance-based effective-voter formula under exchangeable/equal-competence assumptions and a small MMLU proof-of-concept. Two preprints add output-derived process-diversity prediction and a capability-controlled audit showing that several diversity metrics are confounded with model strength. ICML 2026 evidence on D-FUSEr and HiddenBench adds, respectively, an error-shaping intervention and a warning that complementary private information is not automatically elicited. SAC remains conditional on receiver evaluation and graph assumptions.

The proposed Resilience-Aware Organ Population uses a capability map, task/failure-specific co-failure map and dependency/provenance graph. The next milestone is an organ-independence audit followed by common-mode fault injection and repair tests under shared-dependency compromise.
