# Contradictions — Pass 15 Addendum

## C15-1: Communication gain vs interaction tax

Selective communication papers report gains, while the Interaction Tax reports that full-solution interaction can erase diversity under matched budgets. These are not necessarily incompatible: the discriminating variable appears to be which information is exposed and when, not communication itself.

**Research implication:** compare passive broadcast, full-solution exchange, disagreement-preserving exchange, and active information requests under equal communication budgets.

## C15-2: Private information vs private-channel risk

Private channels can preserve specialist-specific information, but Colosseum shows that secret channels can also facilitate collusion. Constraint-drift work shows constraints can degrade during delegation and communication.

**Research implication:** privacy from other agents is not the same as secrecy from the governance layer. Provenance and auditable channel metadata are required even when message contents remain hidden.

## C15-3: Adaptive coalition formation vs epistemic independence

Coalition formation can improve stability, while dense coupling can collapse diversity. Stability and epistemic independence are therefore potentially competing objectives rather than one monotonic property.

**Research implication:** measure coalition stability and independent-information retention jointly; do not optimize either in isolation.

## C15-4: Value of information vs value of disagreement

Classical/decision-theoretic VoI tends to favor information that improves expected utility. Diversity-aware debate work suggests that preserving disagreement can itself have future option value because premature convergence destroys unexplored hypotheses.

**Research implication:** the scheduler should value both immediate information gain and preservation of future search diversity.
