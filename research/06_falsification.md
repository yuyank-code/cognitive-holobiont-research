# Falsification Framework

The architecture must be exposed to experiments capable of disproving its central claims.

## Latent communication

A latent bridge should not be considered successful merely because the receiver improves. Controls must determine whether the information was genuinely transmitted, already present in the receiver, leaked through shared training data, or reproduced by ordinary ensemble effects.

## Specialization

Compare independently trained specialists, shared-backbone specialists, MoE experts, and Holobiont organs. Measure both task performance and error correlation.

## Consensus

Measure accuracy, calibration, diversity, minority preservation, correlated errors, communication cost, and adversarial susceptibility. Agreement alone is not a success criterion.

## Regeneration

Destroy or remove an organ under controlled conditions. Compare no-repair, ordinary retraining, checkpoint restoration, distillation, hypernetwork reconstruction, and Holobiont reconstruction. Evaluate capabilities, robustness, calibration, OOD behavior, and task-specific performance.

## Common-mode failure

Corrupt shared components independently: backbone, bridge, memory, hypernetwork, routing, constitution. Determine whether redundancy still provides protection.

## Immune system

Introduce benign unusual specialists, naturally uncertain specialists, corrupted specialists, poisoned specialists, and strategically malicious specialists. Measure false quarantine and missed detection rates.

## Recursive modification

Require self-modification to improve a predefined held-out objective while preserving prior capabilities. Test for capability regression, reward hacking, irreversible architectural damage, and deceptive evaluation behavior.

## Novelty test

Before claiming that Holobiont is a new architecture, reproduce the strongest relevant baselines from MoE, multi-agent, ensemble, model-stitching, modular-network, and self-healing literature.
