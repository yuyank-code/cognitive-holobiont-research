# Claim–Evidence Matrix — Pass 38 Addendum
**Date:** 2026-11-23

| Claim / hypothesis | New evidence | Evidence status | What it supports | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| More organs imply more independent protection | Morante & Romero García-Rubio (Discover Informatics, 2026) | Peer-reviewed journal article; theory plus small MMLU proof-of-concept | Correctness correlation caps variance-based effective voter count | General information count or full Holobiont reliability |
| Different model families reduce common-mode errors | Same article; Kim et al. (ICML 2025) | Peer-reviewed evidence with limited task samples | Structural diversity can reduce error correlation relative to clones/personas | Independence; different providers still share substantial errors |
| Output-derived process diversity predicts co-failure | Tieman & Markou (arXiv 2026) | Preprint; 38 models, 10 benchmark families | NCD residual adds predictive association beyond semantic distance and capability | Causal benefit from increasing NCD; recovered internal generative mechanisms |
| Common diversity metrics predict voting gains | Donghwan Kim (arXiv 2026) | Preprint; majority-vote audit on MMLU-Pro/TruthfulQA | Capability confounds several diversity metrics; pairwise co-failure retains a modest residual association | That diversity is useless; results for learned routing, private evidence or fault tolerance |
| Error distributions can be shaped to help aggregation | D-FUSEr (ICML 2026) | Peer-reviewed conference paper | Targeted error-distribution shaping can improve tested aggregation/refinement | Safe or general benefits across tasks and organ populations |
| Complementary private evidence will be integrated automatically | HiddenBench (ICML 2026) | Peer-reviewed conference paper | Current agents often fail to elicit latent private knowledge | That all distributed-information systems fail; structured protocols improve results |
| Receiver-side filtering protects against Byzantine neighbors | SAC (2026 preprint) | Preprint; small graph/task experiments | Receiver-side scoring plus graph assumptions can suppress selected attacks | Truth guarantees; robustness to evaluator common-mode error or adaptive long-horizon attacks |
| Model identity/provenance is enough to infer independence | Combined evidence | Contradicted as a general assumption | Provenance can inform priors and dependency audits | Independence without task-specific failure measurements |
