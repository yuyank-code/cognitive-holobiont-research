# Cognitive Holobiont Research Run — 2026-09-16

## Scope
Substantive follow-up on latent communication, collective reasoning under distributed information, scalable coordination, selection, and latent-state integrity. The treatise remains a hypothesis/specification.

## New evidence

### 1. Causal audit of latent channels
Zhang & Emu (2026), *Do Latent Channels Actually Communicate? A Causal Audit of Latent Multi-Agent LLM*, introduces controlled message replacement tests at the sender/receiver boundary. Their results show that aggregate end-task gains can decompose into information retained from an unrelated example versus genuinely example-specific sender content, with directions changing by model size/task. This is important negative/positive nuance: latent communication effects are real but accuracy deltas alone are not sufficient to identify the mechanism.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26773
Evidence label: Strongly supported that causal auditing is necessary; latent sender-specific transfer remains conditional/mechanism-dependent.

### 2. HiddenBench: distributed information is still not being integrated reliably
Li, Naito & Shirado (ICML 2026), *Systematic Failures in Collective Reasoning under Distributed Information in Multi-Agent LLMs*, reports 30.1% accuracy for MAS versus 80.7% for a single agent given complete information across a 65-task HiddenBench. The proposed diagnosis is failure to reason about latent information asymmetry: agents converge on shared evidence while critical facts remain unrequested/unexpressed. Structured communication improves some cases but does not erase the underlying problem.
Source: https://luneresearch.com/papers/4b8e9367-e5c6-43ba-bad9-1c1ea9e6284e
Evidence label: Strong contradictory evidence against naive collective-integration claims.

### 3. MAS-BENCH confirms scaling/termination failure on algorithmic coordination
Yang et al. (ACL Findings 2026), *When 20 Agents Fail to Sort*, isolates distributed sorting under explicit communication constraints. Success drops as agent count grows, with failures in shared state, convention alignment, and termination. CAMOC's early metadata exchange and single-commit verification substantially improve the benchmark, suggesting coordination structure—not raw model count—is the key variable.
Source: https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1698/
Evidence label: Strong support for explicit coordination/termination layers; contradiction to scale-alone hypothesis.

### 4. Latent communication evidence is broader than Interlat alone
LatentMAS (ICML 2026) reports pure latent collaboration using shared latent working memory, with gains across nine benchmarks and large token reductions. Interlat (ACL 2026) independently reports heterogeneous-model latent communication. StateBridge (2026 preprint) reports training-free hidden-state alignment across four models/two families. Together these strengthen feasibility but still do not establish that latent communication causes genuinely distributed computation.
Sources: https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1248/ ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13317 ; https://luneresearch.com/papers/da9816fd-e99f-4e89-9e1b-f0e013978659
Evidence label: Strongly supported for feasible latent transport; distributed-computation claim unresolved.

### 5. Latent state is also an integrity/security object
Brito & Baquero (2026), *When Latent Agents Lie*, reports that tampered KV state can damage final answers while visible commitments remain plausible; an HMAC-bound transport manifest detected recorded tampering. This makes provenance and transport integrity part of the architecture, not an optional security add-on.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28958
Evidence label: Partially supported/important security evidence; attack generality still needs broader testing.

### 6. Selection and diversity remain coupled bottlenecks
ACL 2026 work on debate finds vanilla MAD can underperform majority vote and that diversity of initial views plus calibrated confidence improves outcomes. Separate ACL 2026 work finds dense interaction can cause diversity collapse, while another controlled study reports single-agent multi-output can exceed MAS in semantic diversity. This reinforces the requirement that Holobiont evaluations separate generator diversity, selection quality, and communication topology.
Sources: https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1694/ ; https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.13/ ; https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1894/

## Updated interpretation
The central hypothesis is now best decomposed as:
1. information transport;
2. capability-aware selection/routing;
3. distributed information acquisition;
4. distributed computation;
5. reliable integration/verification;
6. resilience under independent and correlated failures.

The field has credible evidence for (1), growing methods for (2), and useful protocols for (3)/(5), but current benchmarks provide strong evidence that (4) remains the central unsolved bottleneck.

## Mathematical revision
Replace raw agent-count scaling with a conditional information contribution metric. For receiver R, sender i, input X, receiver-private state Z, and sender message M_i:

I_i = I(Y; M_i | X, Z, M_{-i})

This quantity is intended to isolate information supplied uniquely by sender i after conditioning on what the receiver and other senders already provide. A Holobiont should demonstrate positive aggregate unique contribution:

U = I(Y; M_{1:K} | X,Z) - max_{b in B} I(Y; B | X,Z)

where B ranges over matched centralized/single-agent baselines. This is a research metric, not yet a validated universal statistic. Estimation should use controlled message substitutions, permutation/null messages, self-substitution, and receiver-private-information controls rather than representation probes alone.

For coordination, define a distributed-computation efficiency:

DCE = task_success / (compute + alpha * communication + beta * coordination_rounds)

and require comparison against information/compute-matched centralized and MoE baselines. This prevents “more communication” from being mistaken for more collective computation.

## New falsification tests
- Swap each sender's latent message with another example while holding receiver context fixed; measure sender-example-specific causal effect.
- Hide a subset of facts from every individual agent but make them collectively available; compare MAS with a single model given the same total information and compute.
- Increase agent count at fixed total compute and fixed total communication budget; test whether performance scales with effective independent information rather than headcount.
- Compare dense, sparse, hub, and adaptive topologies under equal bandwidth; measure success, drift, diversity collapse, and common-mode failure.
- Corrupt latent payloads without changing visible commitments; test integrity-aware versus text-only verification.
- Compare single-agent multi-output, MoE, ensemble, conventional MAS, latent MAS, and proposed Holobiont under matched resource budgets.

## Architecture consequence
The Holobiont should not be modeled as “many specialists connected by a latent bus.” The emerging scientifically defensible abstraction is a governed information-acquisition/computation network in which specialists can remain private and independent, communication is selectively initiated, every transfer has provenance and causal value tests, integration can abstain/terminate, and shared infrastructure is explicitly treated as a common-mode risk.

## Confidence changes
- Latent communication feasibility: increased from high/conditional to strong evidence across multiple independent approaches.
- Sender-specific latent information: remains conditional; causal audit makes the evaluation standard stricter.
- Distributed computation from communication: confidence in the negative result increased.
- Need for adaptive communication/termination: increased to high-priority architectural requirement.
- Latent-state integrity/provenance: upgraded from conceptual concern to experimentally demonstrated failure mode.

## Next frontier
Focus on the missing bridge between distributed information acquisition and distributed computation: protocols that cause agents to perform complementary algorithmic subproblems, expose intermediate state with causal provenance, and yield exact or verifiable global results under matched-resource baselines. Do not broaden into implementation until this bridge is theoretically and empirically better specified.
